# AI Manhua Dramas from Zero to One: What a Course Should Deliver

I am designing an in-person course on AI-assisted manhua dramas.

It is not a tour of dozens of AI tools, and it should not promise that every student will make a fixed income after graduation. The more honest goal is simple: help someone who cannot write scripts, draw, or edit video finish a first manhua drama and understand how to keep producing afterward.

## Two products, two promises

The course should be split into two products.

The first is a four-day production sprint. It answers one question: can I actually make something? Students choose a topic, write a script, create characters and storyboards, generate visuals, add voice, edit, subtitle, and publish. The minimum deliverable is one finished episode; the standard target is a three-episode mini-series.

The second is a 20-workday commercial practice camp. It answers a different question: can I keep producing, build an account, and turn the work into a portfolio for jobs or client work? Students finish an account position, reusable character and scene assets, five to ten connected episodes, a publishing and review loop, and a commercial execution plan.

The four-day course creates the certainty of “I made this.” The 20-day course focuses on consistent production and commercial opportunities.

## My actual advantage

I am not a traditional screenwriter or professional illustrator. I come from software engineering, and my strengths are AI tools, automation, and turning complex workflows into executable steps.

That is close to the starting point of most potential students. They are not professional writers either. They may be changing careers, building a content account, or trying to find a practical entry point into AI.

The course therefore teaches a one-person production pipeline:

1. Topic: find conflict, reversals, and a story that can continue;
2. Writing: use AI to develop the outline, characters, and dialogue;
3. Visuals: build reusable character, scene, and shot assets;
4. Production: create visuals, voice, editing, and subtitles;
5. Business: publish, review, build a portfolio, and look for jobs or clients.

Tools will change. The path from an idea to a finished work and then to a commercial opportunity is more durable.

## The four-day path

Day one covers the topic, characters, and the first three scripts. Beginners can start with urban power fantasies, workplace reversals, emotional revenge stories, or light mysteries because the conflicts are clear and feedback is fast.

Day two creates character and scene masters plus storyboards, with special attention to visual consistency.

Day three completes the first episode: visuals, voice, editing, subtitles, sound effects, and pacing.

Day four extends the workflow to episodes two and three, packages the account for publishing, and reviews the opening hook, conflict, ending, and data.

The result is not a count of lessons watched. It is a work the student can take away: one finished episode at minimum, three connected episodes as the standard, and five or more for strong performers.

## No promise of virality, but a promise of output

I do not yet have a personal viral manhua-drama case to show. The course should not invent play counts or use “guaranteed income” as a promise.

The stronger approach is to create three pilot works before launch: a workplace reversal, an emotional power fantasy, and a light mystery. Each should show the full path from brief to script, characters, storyboard, and final video. A few non-professionals can then test whether they can follow the workflow.

The real question is not whether I am already a viral creator. It is whether an ordinary person can finish a work from zero with this method.

## From livestream to business outcome

The enrollment path has three steps: demonstrate one complete AI manhua drama in a livestream; use the four-day course as a low-friction production experience; then invite students with finished work and strong execution into the 20-day program.

The meaningful metrics are completion, published works, upgrades, referrals, and continued publishing after 30 days—not just registrations.

The product is not “I know many AI tools.” It is a practical path that breaks a task once reserved for a writer, artist, voice actor, and editor into steps an ordinary person can execute.
